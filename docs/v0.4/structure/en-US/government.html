Let citizens recognize the service asking for their trust.

A public agency must know who is using its service. A citizen should also have a way to verify the interaction before sharing access or approving a sensitive request.

A service people can recognize.

An official appearance is only the beginning.

People may use a public-service portal only a few times a year. A familiar seal or an urgent message can look convincing even when the destination is unfamiliar. Employees and contractors face similar questions at everyday access points.

With the app-based FDA approach, the enrolled app verifies the authentication server associated with the connected service. The person checks the request presented across the two screens. Verification has a role for the agency and for its user.

Put confirmation before a sensitive account change.

Consider a citizen service changing the contact details used to manage account access. The application can be configured to hold that change and request NoPass confirmation from the account holder’s device before proceeding.

The agency retains its identity-proofing, authorization and service rules; the approval checkpoint supplies a person’s decision within that workflow.

Make the next action clear.

A recognizable request and a clear choice help people understand what the service is asking them to do. The same principle can guide workforce access for agency employees and contractors.

Discuss a citizen or workforce interaction with the team, including the access and assisted-support needs of its users.

Account authentication and identity proofing are different tasks.

The public service owns citizen enrollment, eligibility and identity-proofing requirements. FDA authenticates the enrolled relationship; it does not establish a legal identity or confer a NIST assurance level.
