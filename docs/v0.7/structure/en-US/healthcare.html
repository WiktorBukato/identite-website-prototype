The request should feel connected to care.

A patient receives a prompt to open a portal. A clinician returns to a workstation between tasks. Familiar names and repeated sign-ins can make it easy to act before considering who is asking.

A sensitive portal request deserves a deliberate decision.

Consider a patient portal that asks the account holder to authorize release of a personal health record. The portal can be integrated with a NoPass™ approval checkpoint: hold the release, request device confirmation and apply the person’s response.

The portal retains its consent and access rules. Its integration determines where this confirmation belongs before confidential information is released.

Plan consent and recovery around care.

Define who may request a record release, how patient consent is recorded, and how access is recovered when a device is lost. An authentication result does not replace the portal’s consent, authorization or clinical policy.
