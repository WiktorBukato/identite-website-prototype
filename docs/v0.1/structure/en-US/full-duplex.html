How it works / Full Duplex Authentication

How does your user know it is really you?

An organization needs to authenticate its user. The person also needs a reason to trust the service asking for approval. Full Duplex Authentication makes that relationship work in both directions.

A relationship both sides can check.

A familiar appearance is not a verified relationship.

A logo, a convincing message or a familiar-looking sign-in page can invite trust before the person knows who is behind it. Removing a password is useful, but the person still needs to recognize the service and the request they intended.

FDA starts from a relationship established between the connected service, the authentication server and the user’s enrolled app.

Both sides take part in establishing trust.

The picture is the visible part of that exchange. The app’s server verification gives the comparison its context; the person’s decision connects the interaction to their intent.

The organization verifies the enrolled user.

The authentication server checks the enrolled credentials and the factors configured for the request.

The app verifies its authentication server.

The enrolled app checks its established server relationship before presenting the visual comparison to the person.

Trust also matters after sign-in.

A person may be signed in while a particular operation still needs their approval. NoPass can request a fresh confirmation on the phone at that point. The application holds the operation and uses the response in its execution policy.

Authentication establishes the account relationship. Application permissions decide what that account may do; an operation-approval step asks for the person’s decision when it matters.
